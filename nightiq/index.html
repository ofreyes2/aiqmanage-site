Your night, measured on device NEW

Your breathing went quiet 47 times last night. The longest gap lasted 22 seconds. You snored for two hours and fourteen minutes, and the loudest stretch was just before 2 a.m. That is the kind of thing you sleep straight through and never find out. NightIQ records the night with your iPhone's microphone and tells you what it heard.

Audio never leaves your phone. This is the part most sleep apps get wrong, so it is worth being specific. NightIQ has no account, no login, no server, and no network code. Audio is analysed on your iPhone, as it is captured, using Apple's on-device sound classifier plus our own signal processing. It is never uploaded, never streamed, never backed up to a cloud, and never sent to us, because there is no “us” to send it to. We could not read your nights if we wanted to.

You are recording yourself asleep. That should stay yours.

What you get in the morning

  • Quiet gaps. How many times the sound of your breathing dropped away, and for how long. The count, the longest one, and where each one falls in the night.
  • Snoring. Total time, how loud, and the stretches where it was worst.
  • A timeline of the whole night. Scrub through it and see how the quiet gaps and the snoring line up with each other, hour by hour.
  • Trends. One night is a data point. Thirty nights is a pattern. NightIQ keeps a running baseline so you can see whether a change actually changed anything, or whether it was just a bad Tuesday.
  • Notes. Log the things that might matter. A late drink, a cold, a new pillow, a different room. Then look back and see whether any of it moved the numbers.

How it works

Plug the phone in, put it on the nightstand, tap Start, and go to sleep. NightIQ keeps listening with the screen black. In the morning, stop it and read the report. There is a Method and Sources screen, one tap from every report, that explains exactly how each number is produced and what can throw it off.

Honest about the limits

NightIQ hears sound. It does not measure airflow, breathing effort, or blood oxygen, and it does not pretend to. A microphone on a nightstand can tell you that your breathing went quiet, and it can tell you how often. It cannot tell you why.

Results are affected by a bed partner, pets, a fan, traffic, alcohol, a cold, and some medications. NightIQ tells you when a night was noisy or when recording was interrupted, rather than quietly reporting a number it is not confident about.

Important — what NightIQ is and is not

NightIQ is a general wellness app for tracking sleep sounds. It is not a medical device and has not been reviewed or cleared by the FDA or any other regulatory authority. NightIQ analyses sound. It does not measure airflow, breathing effort, or blood oxygen, and it cannot detect, screen for, or diagnose sleep apnea or any other medical condition. It is not a substitute for a sleep study, a home sleep test, or professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Results can be affected by a bed partner, pets, fans, traffic, alcohol, illness, and medication. Never disregard professional medical advice, or delay seeking it, because of something you saw in this app. If you have any concern about your sleep, your snoring, or your breathing, talk to a licensed healthcare professional.
